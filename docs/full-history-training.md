# Full-history global baseline fitting

The requested full-product fit uses the cleaned Online Retail II history through 2011-12-09. Product codes remain distinct, including case variants. Training uses all eligible windows for each included product rather than the earlier 180-window limit. The last 28 historical days form an internal early-stopping slice; short histories cannot supply every model. SKU/day panel gaps between first and last sale are filled as recorded zero sales under the existing source-coverage assumption; no post-last-sale zeros are added. The raw dataset has no stock availability, so sales may be censored.

The global LightGBM uses historical sales-pattern features and calendar features on CPU. The global LSTM uses a 90-day sales sequence and historical 90-day quantity-weighted price with a missingness indicator, trained in mixed-product batches on GPU. The full LSTM uses batch size 512 (up from the 64-product-window batches used in feature-selection validation) to make the larger fit tractable; it is a different training setting and does not inherit the validation score as a guarantee. Both models reserve later historical records for internal early stopping. Historical price at every example is computed only from transactions on or before the forecast origin. The price file stores only aggregate per-product/day units and revenue; the zero invoice/geography fields are placeholders and are not model features.

Models and machine-readable run metadata are stored under ignored `artifacts/full-history-2011-12-09/`; see `manifest.json` for status, exact training times, GPU measurements, product/window counts, hashes and save/reload verification once complete. The output model artifacts are local and are not automatically committed to Git. The previous validation results remain useful for comparing previously trained variants, but training these models through December 2011 includes the proposed held-out dates. Consequently those dates are no longer an untouched final test for the full-history models, and there is no new unbiased accuracy estimate for them. The data is from 2009–2011 and forecasts are not current retail predictions.

The GPU procedure uses a 1 GiB PyTorch allocator cap and a device-wide 1,900 MiB watchdog, with sampled device telemetry. CUDA/driver allocations outside the PyTorch allocator and brief peaks between samples cannot be bounded perfectly by these measures; reports describe observed measurements rather than a guaranteed hardware-wide cap.
