# Forecast feature experiments

The sales-only track uses observed unit quantities. LightGBM constructs lag 1, 7 and 28, means over 7 and 28 days, 28-day standard deviation, the fraction of positive sales days over 7 and 28 days, and days since the most recent nonzero sale (capped at 28). Recursive forecasts feed model predictions back into these features; for zero-day and frequency features, any strictly positive prediction is treated as a sale, a limitation for intermittent-demand forecasting. The LSTM takes only the past 90 normalized, log-transformed sales quantities.

The separate calendar-enhanced LightGBM adds known target-date weekday and month sine/cosine encodings, meteorological season, the England-and-Wales bank-holiday indicator, and days until the next such holiday (capped at 29). The dataset includes international customers, so England-and-Wales holidays are a retailer-location proxy, not customer-specific holidays. Season and holiday features are not causal claims. Calendar features are known at the forecast date; no future realized sales, prices or stock levels are used. LSTM calendar covariates are not yet implemented.

The raw dataset also contains product description, unit price, country, invoice and customer ID. Historical price, regional segmentation and description-derived categories are candidates, not current model features. Product category is not supplied directly. A future category experiment must use descriptions available by each forecast cutoff, document the labeling method, audit misclassifications and avoid treating product identifiers as numeric values. Price is only allowable when observed before the cutoff or explicitly known in advance. Avoid using customer IDs without a clear business need and privacy review.

## Product descriptions, codes, price and country: EDA findings

As of 2011-04-30, the two exploratory cohorts contain 200 products. All 200 have a nonempty description and positive observed price in their earlier records; 33 have more than one normalized description and 195 have more than one distinct price. Across those selected products, common description words include BAG, PACK, GLASS, CANDLE, RED and PINK. These are sample-specific descriptive counts, not dataset-wide estimates. Country is the buyer's destination, not a production country. The top country is the United Kingdom, but multi-country records mean a UK calendar is only a proxy.

UCI defines StockCode as a product identifier and Description as the product name; it does not document an encoding of manufacturer, production country, category or quality in the identifier. Treat codes as categorical identities. A numeric prefix or suffix may be useful for grouping only after external or empirical validation, not as a continuous magnitude or proof of product family. Sources: [UCI dataset documentation](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/502/online+retail+ii); [research on extracting product attributes from text](https://kdd.org/exploration_files/6-Ghani.pdf).

The preliminary `product_features.snapshot` function creates a pre-cutoff catalogue: literal description-word flags, code format, recent sales-weighted unit price, counts of distinct invoices and the proportion of units shipped to UK customers. The flags are indicators that words appear in a title; they are neither validated product categories nor inferred quality. A 90-day price summary describes paid prices and is not a planned future price; price levels may reflect different order sizes or customer discounts. Invoice count is a historical transaction count; invoice numbers are never modeled as numeric features. No customer ID is saved. Run `scripts/build_product_features.py` after the cohort selection script. These are feature-engineering candidates and have not been integrated into training. The 200-SKU catalogue must be reviewed for misclassification, changing descriptions, missingness and leakage before use; any learned vocabulary or category classifier must be fit only on training data.

The code review also found an illustrative candidate variant pair: 35095A is described as BLUE VICTORIAN FABRIC OVAL BOX and 35095B as RED VICTORIAN FABRIC OVAL BOX. A shared numeric base and alphabetic suffix are therefore worth auditing as possible product-family and variant signals, but this example does not establish a general coding rule. `candidate_code_base` records this pattern for review, not as a verified category or a continuous number. `candidate_pack_size` extracts explicit patterns such as `SET/6`, `S/3`, `SET 5` or `PACK OF 12`, returning missing for ambiguous or absent matches. Both require manual review against source descriptions before model use.

## Sample and unique-value review

A fixed-seed sample of 20 out of 200 products is saved locally in `reports/generated/product-feature-sample.csv`; full missingness, unique counts and low-cardinality value frequencies are in `product-feature-profile.json`. The executed notebook `notebooks/02_product_feature_review.ipynb` displays both. Among these 200 SKUs, 170 codes are numeric and 30 have a numeric base with letters; four numeric bases have multiple observed suffix variants (11 products total). This supports investigating families, not assuming the pattern always holds. Candidate pack size is recognized for 18 products and missing for 182, with observed values 6, 12, 20, 35, 60 and 72. Missing means the conservative regex did not recognize a pattern; it is not a measured single-item pack. In the sample, `PACK OF 12 WOODLAND TISSUES` and `PACK OF 60 DINOSAUR CAKE CASES` are parsed as 12 and 60; `60 TEATIME FAIRY CAKE CASES` is an apparent missed candidate. `BAG 125G SWIRLY MARBLES` demonstrates why a word indicator does not automatically equal a product category. These are qualitative spot checks, not measured extraction precision or recall.

## Full-catalogue EDA at the April 2011 cutoff

Run `scripts/build_product_features.py --all`, then `scripts/review_product_features.py --all`; use `scripts/refresh_description_features.py` only after modifying description rules, before reprofiling. The full-catalogue notebook is `notebooks/03_full_product_feature_review.ipynb`. Its 50-product sample is deterministic (seed 42), and the JSON profile lists unique counts, missingness, low-cardinality value counts and numeric quantiles.

Of 4,894 retrospectively identified panel codes, 4,366 have a usable description by the cutoff and 528 do not. `description_available` explicitly separates unknown descriptions from negative keyword matches; word flags are null when a description is missing. The 90-day weighted price is unavailable for 1,945 codes, including products without qualifying sales during that window. Numeric-only codes total 3,391, numeric-with-letter-suffix codes 1,486 and other forms 17. There are 401 repeated candidate numeric bases covering 1,337 suffixed codes; they are only pattern-based hypotheses and include product codes observed after the cutoff. The expanded explicit S/n, SET n and spelled-out number rules produce 402 candidate sizes in the retrospective catalogue; these are not verified ground truth. Missing results mean unknown, not necessarily single units.

The 50-item review includes missed patterns such as `4 PEAR BOTANICAL DINNER CANDLES` and `SEAGRASS S/3 SQU LAUNDRY BOX`; we do not automatically interpret every leading number or abbreviation as pack size. Obvious word plurals such as BOXES and CANDLES are mapped to literal singular-word flags. Names like BAG CHARM remain semantically ambiguous. These observations are qualitative; no labeled holdout or measured precision/recall has yet been established. Broader taxonomy design and annotation remain unfinished.

The code universe was assembled from the complete dataset, including products first observed after April 2011. The extracted descriptions and prices are cutoff-safe values, but the existence of codes in this retrospective catalogue is not cutoff-safe for cohort selection or training. Recreate features and eligible product lists using data available at each forecast origin before model use.

A [review log](reviews/README.md) records the development and held-out spot checks, sample construction, ambiguity and limitations. `scripts/materialize_metadata.py` creates separate as-of validation-cutoff feature snapshots for the previously selected cohorts; it does not fit or evaluate models. `snapshot(..., require_observed=True)` excludes codes without an eligible sale by the cutoff. Tests check that future events do not alter past features.

See [model-specific experiments](model-specific-experiments.md) for the planned ablation and truncation comparisons; the meaning of truncation is being clarified.

A separate 50-description random holdout and its limitations are shown in `notebooks/04_feature_validation.ipynb` and [the review log](reviews/README.md). The held-out phrase `EIGHT PIECE SNAKE SET` was missed; the parser was not tuned to that example. The four validation-cutoff snapshots were refreshed after the rule change, and `scripts/feature_manifest.py` records their source and output hashes.
